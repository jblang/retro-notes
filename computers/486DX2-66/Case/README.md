# Baby AT Desktop Case

![original](original.jpg)

Baby AT desktop case with 3 external 5.25" bays, 1 external 3.5" bay, and 1 internal 3.5" bay.

## Personal Notes

I bought this case in 1995 from my high school's science teacher and IT guy, Mr. Granger, who also owned [Naccom Network Services](https://web.archive.org/web/20020609182758/http://www.naccom.com/home.htm).  The case is unbranded, but the power supply that came preinstalled is a [Hipro 200W model](../Hipro-200W/README.md). Both the case and the PSU were made in Thailand. Here is a list of systems the case has housed over the years:

### Original Pentium-120

The case originally housed a Pentium system that I built from parts after the Dell 486 I got for my birthday in 1993 got hit by lightning.  I took several parts that still worked from the dead 486 and bought the rest from mail order ads in Computer Shopper to build the following system:

- This case and power supply (new)
- Biostar MB-8500TEC motherboard (new)
- [Pentium-120 P54C CPU](../../../parts/cpu/Intel-P54C-120-SX994/README.md) (new)
- 16MB EDO RAM (new)
- Diamond Stealth 64 video card (new)
- Sound Blaster AWE32 (from 486)
- [Sony 2X CD-ROM drive](../../../parts/storage/Sony-CDU55E/README.md) (from 486)
- Canon combo floppy drive (from 486)
- Fujitsu 1GB hard drive (new)

### Spare Parts Pentium-120

Between my freshman and sophmore year in college, I scavenged several parts out of my original Pentium system for the new K6 system I was building. I cobbled together a working system from the remaining parts in this case, plus some other spare parts I had on hand:

- This case and power supply (from Pentium)
- Biostar MB-8500TEC motherboard (from Pentium)
- [Pentium 120MHz P54C CPU](../../../parts/cpu/Intel-P54C-120-SX994/README.md) (from Pentium)
- [8MB FPM RAM](../../../parts/memory/Samsung-4MB-FPM/README.md) (from 486)
- [DFI VG-3000 video card](../../Zenith-Z-248/DFI-VG3000/README.md) (from 386SX)
- [Sony 2X CD-ROM drive](../../../parts/storage/Sony-CDU55E/README.md) (from 486)
- Canon combo floppy drive (from 486)
- [320MB Western Digital hard drive](../../../parts/storage/Caviar-2340-340MB/README.md) (from 486)

After that summer, I went back to college and forgot about the system for nearly 20 years until I found it in my parent's storage and brought it home.

### Reconstructed Pentium-120

When I tried to get the system running again in 2017, the RTC clock battery on the motherboard had died and the computer wouldn't boot without it.  I damaged the motherboard while trying to desolder and replace the clock chip, and I was unable to get the combo floppy drive working again, so I ended up keeping only the case, PSU, and CD-ROM, while buying other parts to rebuild a system very similar to the original:

- Micropolis M54Hi-Plus Motherboard
- [Pentium 120MHz P54C CPU](../../../parts/cpu/Intel-P54C-120-SY033/README.md)
- 32 MB EDO RAM
  - [2x 8MB Hitachi EDO DRAM](../../../parts/memory/Hitachi-8MB-EDO/README.md)
  - [2x 8MB Mitsubishi EDO DRAM](../../../parts/memory/Mitsubishi-8MB-EDO/README.md)
- [Diamond Stealth 64 video card](../../../parts/video/Diamond-Stealth64/README.md)
- Onboard Creative Vibra16 sound chip
- [Mitsumi 8X CD-ROM drive](../Mitsumi-CRMC-FX810T4/README.md)
- [Bytecc BT-145 3.5" 1.44MB floppy drive](../Bytecc-BT-145/README.md)
- [Syba IDE to CF Adapter](../Syba-IDE-CF-Adapter/README.md)

### 486DX2-66

In 2025, I damaged the southbridge on the Micronics motherboard when I dropped a multimeter probe into an ISA slot while it was powered on.  It still works but it doesn't recognize ISA cards anymore. Since I already had another Socket 7 system with the [Junniper Valley K6 system](../../Juniper-Valley-K6/README.md), I decided to go full circle and built the [486DX2-66 system](../README.md) this case currently houses.
